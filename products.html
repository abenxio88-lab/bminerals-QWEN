Iron Ore

High-Grade Hematite for Steelmaking Excellence

Sourced from the mineral-rich deposits of Chiniot and Balochistan, our iron ore is primarily hematite (Fe₂O₃) with exceptional iron content, low impurities, and consistent quality—making it ideal for blast furnace operations and direct reduction processes.

Geological Origin & Composition

Our iron ore originates from Precambrian-aged banded iron formations (BIFs) in the Chiniot region and eastern Balochistan. These deposits formed over 2.5 billion years ago through the precipitation of iron-rich minerals in ancient marine environments, creating some of the highest-grade hematite deposits in South Asia.

The primary mineral is hematite (Fe₂O₃), with minor occurrences of magnetite (Fe₃O₄). The ore body is characterized by its coarse-grained texture, which facilitates efficient crushing, screening, and beneficiations processes.

Mineral Type: Hematite (Fe₂O₃) with minor magnetite

Geological Age: Precambrian (2.5+ billion years)

Reserve Classification: JORC-compliant proven reserves

Chemical Specifications

Our iron ore undergoes rigorous laboratory testing at every stage—extraction, processing, and before shipment. Each batch is analyzed for iron content, silica, alumina, phosphorus, sulfur, and loss on ignition (LOI) to ensure consistent quality that meets international steelmaking standards.

Applications & Industries

Iron ore is the backbone of the global steel industry, and our high-grade material serves multiple applications across the metals value chain. From integrated steel plants to sponge iron production, our ore is optimized for efficient smelting and high yield.

Blast Furnace Feedstock: Primary raw material for pig iron production in integrated steel plants

Direct Reduced Iron (DRI): Used in sponge iron production via gas-based and coal-based processes

Sinter & Pellet Production: Agglomerated for improved furnace permeability and efficiency

Construction Steel: Rebar, structural steel, and wire rod manufacturing

Barite

API-Standard Barite for Global Oil Drilling

Sourced from the Khuzdar district of Balochistan, our barite (barium sulfate, BaSO₄) is processed to meet API RP 13B-1 specifications—the gold standard for drilling mud additives in the global oil and gas industry. With a specific gravity of 4.20+ and 95%+ purity, our material delivers the density and performance that drilling operations demand.

Barite is critical for increasing the density of drilling fluids, preventing blowouts, and maintaining wellbore stability. Our consistent quality, low soluble salts, and reliable supply chain make us a trusted partner for oilfield service companies across the Middle East and beyond.

Processing & Quality Control

Our barite undergoes a multi-stage processing workflow: primary crushing to reduce run-of-mine material, secondary grinding to achieve target particle size distribution, gravity separation to remove gangue minerals, and final screening to meet API mesh specifications.

Quality control is embedded at every stage. Our on-site laboratory conducts hourly checks for specific gravity, fineness of grind (R200 sieve), moisture content, and soluble alkaline earth metals—ensuring every shipment meets API requirements before dispatch.

Chromite

Metallurgical-Grade Chromite for Stainless Steel

Extracted from the ophiolite belts of Muslim Bagh, our chromite ore (FeCr₂O₄) is the essential raw material for ferrochrome production—the key alloying element that gives stainless steel its corrosion resistance. With 46-48% Cr₂O₃ content and a high chromium-to-iron ratio, our material meets the exacting specifications of global ferrochrome smelters.

Chemical Composition

Cr₂O₃: 46-48% | Cr:Fe Ratio: 2.8:1

Our chromite ore is characterized by its high chromium oxide (Cr₂O₃) content, favorable chromium-to-iron ratio, and low impurity levels. These properties make it ideal for charge chrome and ferrochrome production in submerged arc furnaces (SAF).

The Muslim Bagh ophiolite complex hosts some of the purest chromite deposits in Pakistan—formed through the crystallization of ultramafic magmas in the Earth's upper mantle, then exposed through tectonic uplift over millions of years.

Primary Applications

Where Chromite is Used

Ferrochrome Production: 90% of chromite is used to produce ferrochrome, the essential alloy that gives stainless steel (particularly 300-series) its corrosion-resistant properties.

Refractory Materials: Chromite's high melting point (2,100°C) makes it ideal for manufacturing refractory bricks used in steel furnaces and glass tanks.

Chemical Industry: Sodium dichromate production from chromite is used in leather tanning, wood preservatives, and metal surface treatments.

Reserve Information

Muslim Bagh Ophiolite Complex

The Muslim Bagh area hosts one of the largest and most accessible chromite-bearing ophiolite sequences in Pakistan. These ultramafic rock formations represent fragments of ancient oceanic crust, thrust onto the continental margin during the collision of the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates.

Our mining lease covers multiple chromite-bearing zones with proven reserves extending to depths of 150+ meters, ensuring long-term supply security for our clients.

Export Markets

Global Demand

Our metallurgical-grade chromite serves ferrochrome producers and stainless steel manufacturers across China, the Middle East, and Europe. We supply in lump form (10-100mm), chips (5-10mm), and fines (0-5mm) to match smelter requirements.

Annual export capacity of 12,000+ metric tons, with flexible delivery terms including FOB Karachi and CIF destination options.

Copper

Chagai Copper-Gold
Exploration Project

The Chagai district in western Balochistan is emerging as one of the most promising copper-gold exploration frontiers in South Asia. Part of the Tethyan Metallogenic Belt—the same geological province that hosts the world-class Reko Diq and Sain Dek deposits—our exploration concession covers highly prospective ground with confirmed porphyry-style copper mineralization.

Our geological teams are using advanced geophysical surveys, geochemical sampling, and diamond drilling to delineate the extent and grade of copper-gold mineralization. Initial results are promising, with potential for significant resource expansion.

Exploration Status & Potential

While our copper operations are in the exploration phase, the geological potential of the Chagai district is well-documented by decades of regional geological surveys and recent exploration activity by major mining companies.

Geological Setting

The Chagai volcanic arc is part of a Jurassic-aged magmatic belt formed by the subduction of the Neo-Tethyan oceanic crust beneath the Eurasian plate. This tectonic environment is ideal for the formation of porphyry copper-gold deposits—the same geological setting that produces world-class mines in Chile, Indonesia, and Papua New Guinea.

Deposit Type: Porphyry copper-gold with epithermal overprint

Mineralization: Chalcopyrite, bornite, and native gold

Exploration Stage: Systematic drilling and resource definition

Future Production Outlook

Based on current exploration progress, we anticipate moving from resource definition to pilot-scale production within 24-36 months. Initial copper concentrate production is expected to target the growing Chinese and European markets, with potential for downstream cathode copper refining as volumes scale.

The associated gold content in Chagai-style porphyry systems adds significant economic upside, potentially positioning this project as a dual-commodity copper-gold operation.
